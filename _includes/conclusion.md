  <h3 style="color:#ff4500">Conclusion</h3>

  <p>
    So, do birds of a feather flock together or do they end up pecking at each other?
    After exploring Reddit’s dense web of inter-community interactions, we can step back
    and reflect on what similarity really means in this network.
  </p>

  <p>
    Throughout this study, we examined three complementary notions of similarity:
    shared authorship, writing style, and psychological expression. Across all analyses,
    a consistent pattern emerged: communities that are closer in these spaces tend to
    interact more positively, while greater distance is associated with increased
    negativity. This relationship is strongest for shared authorship, weaker for
    psychological similarity, and faint for stylometric similarity.
  </p>

  <p>
    At the same time, our results highlight that Reddit is not a neatly partitioned
    landscape. Subreddits overlap heavily, category boundaries are blurry, and attempts
    to place communities into rigid thematic boxes are not always successful. Similarity on Reddit
    is continuous rather than categorical, shaped by shared users and overlapping
    interests rather than clean divisions.
  </p>

  <p>
    Our category-level analysis and case study revealed dynamics that would have been
    difficult to uncover otherwise. In particular, humour-oriented subreddits showcase a disproportionate
    share of negativity, often directed toward mainstream or topic-focused communities
    rather than toward one another. These subreddits appear to function less as
    participants in discussion and more as commentators of the broader
    Reddit space.
  </p>

  <p>
    Finally, our predictive analysis confirms that similarity does carry information
    about future interactions, but only to a limited extent. Shared authorship is the
    most informative signal on its own, and combining distance measures improves prediction
    slightly, especially using a non-linear model. Still, negativity on Reddit remains only
    partially predictable, reflecting the many forces that shape online conflict.
  </p>

  <p>
    Overall, this work portrays Reddit as a complex and interconnected ecosystem, where
    resemblance can be a source of harmony but does not guarantee it. Our findings invite further research
    into new notions of similarity and how these dynamics evolve over time, especially
    given that our data spans over three years of Reddit’s history.
  </p>
